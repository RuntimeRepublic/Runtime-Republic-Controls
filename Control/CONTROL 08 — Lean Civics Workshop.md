# 1. Purpose

How every Runtime Republic team, at any level and with any AI, rates current taxes and proposed replacements against the AGCL Control Documents, and builds competing solution plans. The workshop is asynchronous and open to an unlimited number of teams. Runtime-Republic-Controls governs all teams.

---

# 2. Core Principle

Every tax is treated the same way, scored the same way, and improved by competing solutions. The goal is a system closer to the ideals, not a claim of perfection.

# 3. Rules

## 3.1 Working rules (how we reason)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| R1 | **Controls first.** Ratings and fixes come from AGCL-Control-Documents (cite control, rule and version). Turn to Scripture (KJV first, per 000 Rule S1) only when a control document fails to answer and the reviewers agree it failed. |
| R2 | **Whole story or nothing (000 Rule S4).** A biblical pattern is used with all its conditions and context, exactly as written, or not at all. |
| R3 | **Outside critics are not the standard.** Witnesses may confirm results (000 Rule S5); they never set the grade. |
| R4 | **Facts, interpretation and proposed reform stay separate.** Unverified items are marked UNVERIFIED. |
| R5 | **Ask before building.** When a request could mean more than one thing, ask one multiple-choice question first. |

## 3.2 Workshop rules (what we hold)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| W1 | **Patterns outlive the tax.** When a tax ends, its pattern stays on record. If a crisis forces taxes back, only a tax of the same pattern that was left behind may return, under the same conditions. |
| W2 | **Exit points can be built in.** Exits are not only dates: a rate that steps down on a published schedule to zero, and a base limited to purchases (no purchase, no tax; buy used; grow your own; ask no one for help). Scored under 00D; a falling rate also fixes the 00A duration. |
| W3 | **Closer to the ideal, not perfect.** A fix succeeds when it raises the score and moves the trail toward visibility. Each step is judged against today's system. |
| W4 | **Forced sinking fund.** A replacement tax steps down on a published schedule (example: 10% falling 0.025 points per step). Government must fund partitioned sinking funds large enough to cover each declared purpose before the rate reaches zero. Sinking funds work only when government is forced to use them. |
| W5 | **Two kinds of tax.** Every tax is classified **existence** (takes property for having or earning it: income, payroll, property tax) or **event** (rides on a voluntary market event: sales, fuel). Existence taxes are replacement targets. Only event taxes may replace them, and only an event pattern may be resurrected (W1). Borderline cases are flagged for the team to rule on. |
| W6 | **Score by rules met** (proposed, not yet adopted). Each control scores the fraction of its applicable rules met. Until adopted, controls score Met 1 / Partial 0.5 / Not met 0. |
| W7 | **Tracking binds government only, and only tax pathways.** Only government takes property without the full elements of a contract (offer, acceptance, consideration, consent), so transparency and ledger duties bind government, not private parties. Ledger or blockchain tracking is limited to the tax pathway (collection → fund → budget line → payee) and never tracks the person who paid. The Regulative Principle binds church and state; beyond what God requires and forbids, individuals are free to innovate. |
| W8 | **Three reasons for secrecy.** Government may withhold detail only for national defense, to protect minors, or to protect victims of crimes. Existence, cost, duration and authority are never secret. Conflicts with 00G Rule G6 (broader classification); proposed amendment to be handled under 00J. |
| W9 | **The people own the tax (00C Rule C7).** Collecting an event tax does not separate ownership. Tax money stays the payers' property, held by government as steward. Pooling, tracking, reversion and sinking-fund rules follow from this. |

## 3.3 The Tax Card (same for every tax)

| Section | Holds | Measured against |
|---|---|---|

## 3.4 Plan ladder and team rules

Federal plans: AGCL-Control-Documents `plan/` (canonical; lower levels never edit it). State and local plans: built by teams, starting from the federal plans. Location to be decided.

| # | Team rule |
|---|---|
| T1 | Same level, same scope: a team plans only within its charter. |
| T2 | Declare overlaps: a plan that touches another level lists those groups and invites their review (example: a state property-tax team names the school-board property-tax group). Ready when each has reviewed or a set window has passed. |
| T3 | A lower plan may go further for liberty than an accepted higher plan, never against it. |
| T4 | Framework test every time: AGCL (rules cited), NBBF and NBEF; Yes / No / Unclear with evidence for and against; and: does it enumerate and limit government power, and protect life, liberty and property? |
| T5 | Same measure everywhere: progress is the rating going up. |
| T6 | Name the replacement: removing a tax names what funds the need or which spending ends, and the transition. Nothing counted twice. |
| T7 | Every plan carries a citizen test card (00L L1, L4). |
| T8 | Version every change (00J). |
| T9 | Solutions compete per gap. Any solution that closes a gap passes; the team picks the better idea, not merely the passing one. |

Unlimited teams: one school district may have parent teams for all three frameworks, all scoring the same way.

**G2 correction (James, 2026-11-23):** G2 is not met for the income tax. Compliance cost (filing, records, preparers) is disclosed nowhere. Social Security and Medicare also hide the employer's half, which is paid for the worker's labor all the same; that fails G2 and B1 for both. Social Security, Medicare and H.R. 25 are to be re-scored by this method.

**Proposed amendment (not yet filed):** 00G G2 to name "compliance cost to all parties: payer, collector or employer, and government administration and enforcement," through the 00J process.

**Witness (Rule S5):** Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations* (1776), Book V, Ch. II, Part II, fourth maxim: a tax should take "as little as possible over and above what it brings into the public treasury." Already listed in AGCL_Doctrinal_Foundations_and_Economic_Lineage.txt.

**H.R. 25 bill findings (119th Congress, as introduced; govinfo):** Sec. 204, sellers' administrative credit of the greater of $200 or 0.25% of tax remitted, capped at 20%; Sec. 401(d)(2), administering states keep 0.25%; Sec. 509, the tax is charged separately and shown on every receipt. Collection costs are declared and paid, consistent with Smith's maxim. Sec. 401 sunset text not yet read.

**Blue team price research (witnesses, not authorities):** Jorgenson (Harvard), embedded taxes in prices (22% figure secondhand, UNVERIFIED); Bachman, Haughton, Kotlikoff, Sanchez-Penalver and Tuerck, NBER Working Paper 12732 (2006), 23% rate feasible, producer prices fall relative to consumer prices (Kotlikoff consulted for FairTax.org); Kotlikoff, Cato Policy Analysis 193 (1993); Poterba (MIT), consumer prices may not rise by the full tax under imperfect competition.

# 5. Edge Cases (must be tested)

| Case | Handling |
|---|---|
| A tax is partly existence, partly event | Flag on the Tax Card; team rules (W5) |
| Two AIs or teams disagree on a score | Show both, rule by rule; the rubric decides |
| A replacement plan raises the score but adds a new existence tax | Fails W5 |
| A lower-level plan conflicts with a higher accepted plan | Fails T3 unless it goes further for liberty |
